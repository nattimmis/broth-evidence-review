## Papers reviewed

### PMID 42878582 — Impact of body composition on clinical outcomes in patients with hepatocellular carcinoma: A systematic review and meta-analysis

- **Journal:** Hepatology Communications (2026 Nov 1 / epub 2026 Oct 14)
- **Design:** Systematic review and meta-analysis, PRISMA 2020
- **n:** 92 studies included overall; the pooled mortality estimate draws on 44
  studies, the recurrence estimate on 14 studies (k=14). No single aggregate
  patient count given in the abstract — UNVERIFIED at the patient level.
- **Finding:** Sarcopenia independently predicted worse overall survival across
  curative (HR 1.52–3.76), locoregional (HR 1.72–2.51) and systemic (HR
  1.34–2.20) treatment strata. Pooled random-effects estimate: HR 1.85 (95% CI
  1.75–1.94, I²=0%, 44 studies). Sarcopenia also predicted recurrence after
  curative treatment: pooled HR 1.71 (95% CI 1.61–1.82, k=14). Visceral
  adiposity was protective during systemic therapy (HR 0.56–0.66) but
  detrimental after curative treatment — a split, not a single clean effect.
- **Classification:** STRENGTHENS / EXTENDS
- **Reasoning:** `docs/02-cirrhosis-nutrition.md` already states sarcopenia is
  "a hard prognostic factor" and cites the EASL 1.2–1.5 g/kg/day protein
  targets (1.5 g/kg with sarcopenia), with a general cirrhosis/cancer-cachexia
  framing. This meta-analysis is HCC-specific, much larger (92 studies) and
  gives a tight pooled mortality HR with no heterogeneity (I²=0%) across every
  treatment modality — it sharpens the existing claim with harder, HCC-specific
  numbers rather than changing it. The adiposity split (protective in systemic
  therapy, harmful after curative treatment) is new detail not currently in
  the doc, but it is a nuance about body-fat distribution under active cancer
  treatment, not a diet recommendation this page makes, so it does not change
  anything the page currently tells this person to eat or not eat.

### PMID 42876044 — Global Underutilization of Antiviral Therapy and Survival Outcomes in Hepatitis B Virus-Related Hepatocellular Carcinoma: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis of 78 Studies and 68,243 Patients

- **Journal:** Journal of Viral Hepatitis (2026 Nov)
- **Design:** Systematic review and meta-analysis, PRISMA
- **n:** 78 studies, 68,243 patients (52 studies / 41,345 patients for the
  treatment-rate estimate)
- **Finding:** Antiviral treatment rate pooled at 53.1%; treated patients had
  higher 3- and 5-year overall survival than untreated (80.4% vs 59.2% and
  72.1% vs 46.8%, both p<0.01). No significant difference in HCC recurrence
  rates.
- **Classification:** NOT RELEVANT
- **Reasoning:** This is specifically about hepatitis-B-virus-related HCC and
  antiviral (nucleos(t)ide analogue) treatment for that viral aetiology. This
  person's liver disease is alcohol-related, not HBV-related — wrong
  aetiology, no HBV antiviral indication applies, and the page makes no claims
  about antiviral therapy. Textbook "does not bear on this person" case.
