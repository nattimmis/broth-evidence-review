## Papers

### PMID 41837582 — Effect of dietary fibre supplementation on metabolic endotoxemia: a systematic review and meta-analysis of randomised clinical trials
*Int J Food Sci Nutr, 2026 May; meta-analysis, 15 RCTs, n=773*
Dietary fibre supplementation significantly reduced serum lipopolysaccharide (p<0.00001) and TNF-α (p<0.02), and increased IL-10 (p<0.02); no significant effect on LPS-binding protein, CRP, IL-6, HDL, or LDL. Population is general/metabolic-endotoxemia cohorts, not cirrhosis-specific, and no cirrhosis-specific timeframe is given in the abstract.
**STRENGTHENS/EXTENDS** — doc 06 already cites an oat beta-glucan meta-analysis and makes the mechanistic point that fibre binds bile acids, not toxins, while being honest that no trial of oats exists in cirrhosis. This is a new, larger synthesis on the fibre→endotoxin-marker link specifically (LPS/TNF-α down, but note the null results on LBP/CRP/IL-6 too) — consistent with, and a good additional citation for, doc 06's existing framing. It does not establish this in cirrhotic patients, so it should not be used to claim a cirrhosis-specific effect.

### PMID 42470041 — Preoperative albumin-to-globulin ratio as a prognostic factor after curative hepatectomy for HCC
*Medicine (Baltimore), 2026 Jul; meta-analysis, 6 studies, n=2,107, Asian populations*
Low AGR associated with worse overall survival (HR 1.69, 95% CI 1.37–2.08) and recurrence-free survival (HR 1.62, 95% CI 1.37–1.91) after curative hepatectomy.
**NOT RELEVANT** — a prognostic biomarker for patients undergoing curative surgical resection. Not applicable to someone with stage 4 disease who is not a curative-hepatectomy candidate, and the page makes no claims about surgical biomarkers.

### PMID 42461487 — Routine lymph node dissection in primary liver tumour surgery
*J Gastrointest Cancer, 2026 Jul; meta-analysis, 40 studies, 5,872 patients*
Pooled lymph node metastasis incidence 5% in HCC (n=601), 37% in intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma, 39% in perihilar cholangiocarcinoma; LNM associated with worse survival in the latter two but authors conclude routine dissection should not be performed for HCC.
**NOT RELEVANT** — a surgical staging/technique question for resection candidates; not applicable and not a claim the page makes.

### PMID 42379830 — Direct-acting antivirals in HCV-related HCC
*Gut Liver, 2026 Jul; meta-analysis, 88 studies, n=8,839*
DAA treatment associated with lower recurrence (HR 0.47, 95% CI 0.32–0.70) and lower all-cause mortality (HR 0.40, 95% CI 0.31–0.51) in HCV-related HCC.
**NOT RELEVANT** — wrong aetiology. This person's liver disease is alcohol-related, not hepatitis C, and the page makes no HCV/antiviral claims.

### PMID 42299577 — Local treatment (TACE/HAIC) plus immunotherapy and targeted therapy vs immunotherapy/targeted therapy alone in HCC
*Medicine (Baltimore), 2026 Jun; meta-analysis, 15 studies, n=3,208*
Adding TACE or HAIC improved disease control rate (RR 1.27), objective response (RR 1.87), progression-free survival (HR 0.59) and overall survival (HR 0.48) versus systemic therapy alone, with more toxicity.
**NOT RELEVANT** — a systemic-oncology treatment-selection question for the treating oncologist, not a diet/lifestyle claim the page addresses.

### PMID 42423535 — Environmental endocrine-disrupting chemicals and MASLD
*Liver Int, 2026 Aug; meta-analysis, 42 studies*
Several EDC classes (phthalates, bisphenols, POPs, mercury, lead, cadmium) associated with higher MASLD prevalence; authors note the evidence is cross-sectional and hypothesis-generating, causality not established.
**NOT RELEVANT** — wrong aetiology (metabolic dysfunction-associated disease, not alcohol-related), and environmental-exposure epidemiology is outside anything the page claims.

### PMID 42420603 — Weight loss interventions on liver health in children/adolescents with obesity
*Int J Obes, 2026 Aug; systematic review/meta-analyses, 145 studies, 18,609 participants (10,539 in meta-analysis), ages 5–18*
Weight loss interventions improved liver enzymes and hepatic fat in a majority of studies; bariatric surgery was the only modality improving histology.
**NOT RELEVANT** — wrong population (paediatric) and wrong aetiology (obesity-driven), not applicable to an adult with alcohol-related cirrhosis.

### PMID 41689666 — Dietary interventions on liver health biomarkers in MASLD (systematic review and meta-analysis of RCTs)
*Eur J Nutr, 2026 Feb; meta-analysis, 24 RCTs*
Fasting and Mediterranean diet reduced ALT and liver stiffness (surrogate markers) in MASLD; the authors explicitly note **no eligible MetALD (metabolic + alcohol-related) studies were found**, so findings apply only to MASLD.
**NOT RELEVANT** — the authors themselves rule out the alcohol-related subgroup for lack of data; wrong aetiology, and outcomes are surrogate biomarkers (ALT, liver stiffness) rather than the hard endpoints (fibrosis stage, survival) the page's cirrhosis claims are built on.

### PMID 41935147 — Periodontitis and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease: systematic review and meta-analysis
*Sci Rep, 2026 Apr; meta-analysis, 11 studies (8 cross-sectional, 3 cohort), n=225,091*
Cross-sectional association between periodontitis and NAFLD (OR 1.34, 95% CI 1.13–1.58); limited cohort evidence suggested a possible bidirectional relationship but heterogeneity precluded pooling.
**NOT RELEVANT** — doc 09 already cites stronger, more directly applicable evidence in cirrhosis specifically (periodontal therapy trial improving MELD/cognition in 30 days; periodontitis–cirrhosis mortality HR 2.19, n=184). This new paper is cross-sectional association in NAFLD (wrong aetiology), a weaker design answering a different question than what's already on the page.

### PMID 41559195 — Population attributable fractions of peripheral diseases for dementia burden
*Nat Hum Behav, 2026 Apr; Bayesian meta-analysis + PAF modelling, 202 articles*
Cirrhosis/chronic liver disease contributes an estimated 5.51% (95% CI 1.77–8.86) population attributable fraction to global dementia burden, one of several peripheral-disease contributors.
**NOT RELEVANT** — the outcome (population-level dementia burden) is not something the page makes any claim about; interesting but out of scope.

### PMID 41205638 — Factors influencing severity in herb-induced liver injury: bibliometric and meta-analysis
*J Ethnopharmacol, 2026 Feb; bibliometric/meta-analysis, 382 studies, 643 patients*
Single-herb products were more likely to cause mild injury than mixed-product use; "supplementary product" use was associated with higher cholestasis risk; sex, age, race and usage duration all affected severity and lab markers (GGT, INR, bilirubin).
**STRENGTHENS/EXTENDS** — doc 01 covers turmeric/piperine DILI specifically. This is broader (all herb-induced liver injury, not turmeric-specific) but reinforces the same general caution: mixed-product/supplement formulations carry more risk than single-ingredient culinary use, which is exactly the distinction doc 01 already draws for turmeric ("culinary turmeric only, no piperine supplements"). New-to-the-page citation, not a correction — it doesn't add turmeric-specific numbers.

### PMID 42620506 — Dual-level Bayesian network meta-analysis of first-line immunotherapy in advanced HCC
*Front Immunol, 2026; systematic review/NMA, 15 phase III RCTs, n=9,792*
PD-(L)1 plus VEGF antibody combinations ranked most favourable for overall survival versus TKI monotherapy; atezolizumab+bevacizumab and similar regimens showed OS benefit versus sorafenib.
**NOT RELEVANT** — systemic first-line drug selection for advanced HCC is the treating oncologist's decision; the page does not make claims about immunotherapy regimen choice.

### PMID 41645601 — Network meta-analysis: pharmacological therapies in compensated MASH cirrhosis for fibrosis regression
*Aliment Pharmacol Ther, 2026 Apr; network meta-analysis, 9 RCTs, n=3,266*
In biopsy-proven compensated MASH cirrhosis (F4c), efruxifermin was the only agent significantly superior to placebo for ≥1-stage fibrosis improvement without MASH worsening (SUCRA 77.44); several agents beat placebo for MASH resolution.
**NOT RELEVANT** — wrong aetiology (MASH cirrhosis, not alcohol-related) and these are investigational pharmacotherapies, not diet/lifestyle claims the page makes. Doesn't bear on doc 07's alcohol-abstinence-as-treatment framing or doc 05's food claims.

### PMID 42595456 — Cryoablation versus radiofrequency ablation for HCC: systematic review and meta-analysis
*Int J Hyperthermia, 2026 Dec; meta-analysis, 7 studies, 544 CA vs 925 RFA patients*
Cryoablation had a higher initial complete-ablation rate and less intraoperative pain than RFA, but **no significant difference in progression-free or overall survival** (all comparisons non-significant, p=0.11–0.90) or complication rates.
**NOT RELEVANT** — a locoregional-technique choice for the interventional radiologist/oncologist, not something the page addresses; also worth noting the survival comparison is a clean null result either way.

### PMID 41274499 — Ultra-processed food consumption and long-term risk of MASLD and primary liver cancer
*Clin Nutr ESPEN, 2026 Apr; meta-analysis, 7 cohort studies, n=1,272,317*
Highest UPF consumption associated with higher MASLD risk (HR 1.32, 95% CI 1.11–1.58), but **no significant association with primary liver cancer** (HR 1.21, 95% CI 0.52–2.79), including HCC specifically (HR 0.98, 95% CI 0.66–1.45, essentially null).
**NOT RELEVANT** — the page makes no claim about ultra-processed food as a category, the cohorts are general-population (wrong aetiology for the MASLD signal), and the liver-cancer result — the outcome that would matter most here — is a null. Not a basis for adding a new claim.
