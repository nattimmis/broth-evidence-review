One new PMID appeared in the evidence feed since the last review. It does not change any conclusion on the page: it is a surgical/transplant-technique meta-analysis with a null primary result, and it does not bear on the diet, alcohol-abstinence, or supplement questions this review tracks.

## PMID 42930145

- **Title:** Machine Perfusion and Oncologic Outcomes After Liver Transplantation for Hepatocellular Carcinoma: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis.
- **Journal:** Current Oncology (Toronto, Ont.), 2026 Sep 25;33(10):578.
- **Design:** Systematic review and random-effects meta-analysis of 6 comparative clinical studies (all observational; no RCTs identified). Risk of bias: 3 studies moderate, 3 serious (ROBINS-I).
- **n:** 6 studies pooled (individual patient n not reported in the abstract).
- **Finding:** Comparing machine perfusion vs. standard cold storage during liver transplantation for HCC. Primary outcome, recurrence-free survival: HR 0.34, 95% CI 0.07–1.77, p = 0.20 — **not statistically significant**. Secondary outcome, binary HCC recurrence: RR 0.40, 95% CI 0.13–1.20, p = 0.10 — **not statistically significant**. Some individual studies reported favorable associations with early recurrence, OS, and lower post-op aminotransferase release, but the authors' own pooled estimates are null and they call for further prospective study before any oncologic benefit is established.
- **Classification: NOT RELEVANT.**
- **Reasoning:** This is a question about a graft-preservation technique (machine perfusion vs. cold storage) at the moment of liver transplantation — it has nothing to do with diet, alcohol abstinence, antioxidants, protein/fat sources, oral health, or probiotics, which are what this page's docs (01–14) actually claim. It also targets liver-transplant recipients, a population defined by resectable/transplantable (typically Milan-criteria) disease; "stage 4 cancer" here implies metastatic disease, which is generally a transplant exclusion, so the population doesn't match this person either way. Even setting aside relevance, both pooled estimates in the paper are explicitly non-significant (p = 0.20 and p = 0.10), so even in a world where this population matched, there would be no result to act on — correctly reported here as null, not smoothed into a positive signal.
