# Evidence review — 2026-09-01

Nothing changes a page conclusion this run. Of 8 new PMIDs surfaced since the last

## Papers reviewed

### PMID 40925921 — Exercise and nutrition interventions for sarcopenia in cirrhosis
- **Journal:** European Journal of Clinical Nutrition, 2025 Nov
- **Design:** Systematic review + network meta-analysis (NMA) + pairwise meta-analysis of RCTs
- **n:** 23 trials, 1,525 participants
- **Finding:** By NMA, exercise + protein + BCAA had the greatest effect on muscle mass (SMD 2.12, 95% CI 0.59–3.65). Exercise alone or with supplementation significantly increased muscle mass; **protein or BCAA supplementation alone did not**. By pairwise meta-analysis, exercise significantly improved physical performance vs. control (SMD 1.43, 95% CI 0.63–2.17, p<0.01) and exercise + protein beat protein alone (SMD 0.66, 95% CI 0.26–1.06, p=0.001). BCAA's effect on muscle strength was inconclusive.
- **Classification:** STRENGTHENS / EXTENDS
- **Reasoning:** docs/08-protein-meat-and-fat.md already makes exactly this point — "the same supplement built body protein at night and did nothing in the daytime," plus small exercise+protein trials (n=8, n=40, n=103) — but on single-digit-to-low-hundreds trial evidence. This is a 23-trial, 1,525-participant synthesis confirming the same shape of result (supplementation alone is not enough; exercise is the active ingredient) at much higher confidence. Doesn't change the conclusion, meaningfully upgrades its evidentiary base. Population is cirrhosis generally, not aetiology-specified, but the mechanism (sarcopenia, muscle mass) is directly on-topic.

### PMID 42327777 — First-line treatments for advanced HCC (Bayesian NMA)
- **Journal:** Frontiers in Immunology, 2026
- **Design:** Bayesian network meta-analysis of phase III RCTs
- **n:** 16 trials, 8,753 patients
- **Finding:** HAIC-based regimens (esp. sorafenib+HAIC, HAIC_FO) ranked highest for PFS and OS; several immunotherapy combinations also showed survival benefit without added severe toxicity.
- **Classification:** NOT RELEVANT
- **Reasoning:** This is a comparison of first-line systemic/regional oncology drug regimens for advanced HCC. The page's scope is diet and lifestyle support; it explicitly defers drug and treatment-regimen decisions to the oncology team. No page claim addresses which HCC drug regimen is superior, so there is nothing here to update.

### PMID 42220514 — First-line immunotherapy for advanced HCC, viral-aetiology stratified
- **Journal:** Frontiers in Immunology, 2026
- **Design:** Bayesian network meta-analysis of RCTs
- **n:** 10 trials, 7,301 patients
- **Finding:** Immunotherapy vs. TKI monotherapy improved OS (HR 0.79, 95% CI 0.74–0.84) and PFS (HR 0.70, 95% CI 0.58–0.85) overall. Benefit was more pronounced in HBV-positive patients; in the non-HBV/non-HCV (NBNC) stratum — which is where alcohol-related HCC would sit — the PFS improvement was **not statistically significant** (HR 0.74, 95% CI 0.52–1.06).
- **Classification:** NOT RELEVANT
- **Reasoning:** Same scope issue as above — this is a drug-regimen comparison, not a diet or lifestyle claim, so it's outside what this page asserts. Noted in passing because the NBNC-stratum null PFS result is the subgroup closest to this person's aetiology; that's a fact for the oncology team, not something for this page to house.

### PMID 42038464 — Gut microbiota-regulating drugs for MASLD
- **Journal:** PeerJ, 2026
- **Design:** Systematic review + network meta-analysis of RCTs
- **n:** 27 studies, 1,511 participants
- **Finding:** Probiotics reduced ALT (MD −7.51, 95% CI −12.36 to −2.66) and AST (MD −6.42, 95% CI −11.91 to −0.92) vs. placebo; synbiotics reduced AST, GGT and CAP.
- **Classification:** NOT RELEVANT
- **Reasoning:** Wrong aetiology (MASLD — metabolic — not alcohol-related cirrhosis) and wrong question. The page's probiotic/kefir claim (docs/12) is about **infection risk in an immunocompromised person**, a safety question. This paper measures liver-enzyme efficacy in a non-immunocompromised MASLD population; it says nothing about infection risk and doesn't touch the claim it might appear to.

### PMID 41372862 — Diet modalities for MAFLD (network meta-analysis)
- **Journal:** BMC Gastroenterology, 2025 Dec
- **Design:** Network meta-analysis of RCTs
- **n:** not stated as pooled total; network across 4 diet arms (Mediterranean, intermittent fasting, DASH, low-fat)
- **Finding:** Intermittent fasting best for weight loss (WMD −1.96 kg, 95% CI −3.01 to −0.92); Mediterranean diet best for intrahepatic lipid (WMD −1.68%, 95% CI −3.21 to −0.16) and triglycerides.
- **Classification:** NOT RELEVANT
- **Reasoning:** MAFLD/metabolic population pursuing weight loss — the opposite nutritional problem from a cachectic person with decompensated alcohol-related cirrhosis and stage 4 cancer, for whom the page's targets are calorie and protein **surplus**, not restriction. Wrong population, wrong direction of intervention.

### PMID 41287008 — Drug comparison for NAFLD (Bayesian NMA)
- **Journal:** European Journal of Medical Research, 2025 Nov
- **Design:** Bayesian network meta-analysis of RCTs
- **n:** 26 trials, 2,143 patients
- **Finding:** GLP-1RA ranked best overall; SGLT2 inhibitors showed moderate benefit on weight/liver enzymes; vitamin E "improved liver enzyme levels and histological features" in this NAFLD population.
- **Classification:** NOT RELEVANT
- **Reasoning:** Metabolic NAFLD population, drug-therapy comparison, not diet. The vitamin E result looks adjacent to docs/05's antioxidant-supplement discussion, but the population (metabolic NAFLD, not cirrhotic, not on chemotherapy) is different from both of docs/05's cited studies (PIVENS — biopsy-proven, fibrosis-negative; the chemo-recurrence signal). It doesn't contradict either, because it isn't answering the same question in the same population.

### PMID 40976125 — Chinese patent medicines for liver fibrosis and cirrhosis
- **Journal:** Phytomedicine, 2025 Nov
- **Design:** Network meta-analysis + network pharmacology + animal validation
- **n:** 380 studies, 37,380 patients (network meta-analysis of surrogate biomarkers: HA, LN, IV-C, PC-III, AST, ALT, TBIL)
- **Finding:** Anluo Huaxian Pill (AHP) ranked best for fibrosis; Liuwei Wuling Tablet (LWT) ranked best for cirrhosis, by SUCRA ranking on serum biomarkers.
- **Classification:** NOT RELEVANT
- **Reasoning:** Explicitly a hepatitis-B population ("Hepatitis B virus infection is the primary cause of liver fibrosis" per the abstract), not alcohol-related. Outcomes are serum surrogate markers, not biopsy-confirmed fibrosis regression or survival — the page's fibrosis-regression standard (docs/06/07) is explicitly about hard endpoints at ≥52 weeks, not marker panels. Different drug class (Chinese patent medicines) with no role in this person's care plan.

### PMID 42233597 — Precision pharmacotherapy in MASH (network meta-analysis)
- **Journal:** Clinical Pharmacology & Therapeutics, 2026 Aug
- **Design:** Network meta-analysis of RCTs
- **n:** 69 trials, 16,180 patients
- **Finding:** SGLT2 inhibitors, THR-β agonists, incretins and FGF21 analogs improved fibrosis/MASH resolution vs. placebo. FGF21 analogs particularly effective in cirrhotic MASH (OR 4.25, 95% CI 1.97–9.17).
- **Classification:** NOT RELEVANT
- **Reasoning:** MASH is the metabolic-aetiology disease, not alcohol-related cirrhosis, and this is a drug-therapy comparison, not diet. Wrong aetiology, wrong intervention type.
