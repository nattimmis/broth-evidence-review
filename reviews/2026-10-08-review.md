# Evidence review — 2026-10-08

One new PMID surfaced since the last review, and it does not change anything on the
page: it is a transplant-listing-policy meta-analysis, and this person is almost
certainly not a transplant candidate at all given stage 4 cancer, so the comparison
it makes doesn't bear on their care.

## PMID 42842026

- **Title:** Clinical outcomes of early versus standard liver transplantation in
  patients with alcohol-related liver disease: a meta-analysis.
- **Journal:** Langenbeck's Archives of Surgery, 2026 Oct 2;411(1):266.
- **Design:** Meta-analysis (Cochrane/PRISMA), 3 databases searched (PubMed/Medline,
  Scopus, Cochrane Library).
- **n:** 12 studies, 2,323 patients with alcohol-related liver disease (ALD).
- **Finding:** Compares early liver transplantation (eLT, i.e. transplant before the
  conventional 6-month sobriety requirement) against standard-timing LT (sLT, after
  6 months abstinence).
  - Mortality: RR 0.94, 95% CI 0.73–1.22 — **non-significant**.
  - Patient survival: HR 1.10, 95% CI 0.83–1.45 — **non-significant**.
  - Alcohol relapse: RR 1.37, 95% CI 0.95–2.00 in primary analysis — **non-significant**,
    though it became significant on leave-one-out/subgroup analyses.
  - Acute graft rejection: RR 1.22, 95% CI 0.98–1.52 — non-significant.
  - Graft loss: RR 0.97, 95% CI 0.74–1.27 — non-significant.
  - Post-op infection: RR 1.19, 95% CI 0.80–1.77 — non-significant.
  - Hospitalization: MD +5.24 days for eLT, 95% CI 0.15–10.33 — longer stay with eLT.
  - Authors' conclusion: eLT offers similar survival to sLT and is reasonable "when
    medical therapy fails," but flag higher morbidity/relapse signals and recommend
    careful selection and surveillance.
- **Classification: NOT RELEVANT.**
- **Reasoning:** This is a transplant-candidacy policy question (how long must someone
  abstain before being *listed* for a liver transplant). Active extrahepatic malignancy
  — which stage 4 cancer is — is essentially a universal absolute contraindication to
  liver transplant listing at every major center and in AASLD/EASL guidance; a person
  with stage 4 cancer is not going to be evaluated for eLT or sLT. The paper's own
  findings are also null across every primary endpoint (mortality, survival, graft
  loss, infection), so even in a population where transplant *was* on the table, this
  wouldn't be grounds to change any abstinence- or diet-related claim on the page.
  Nothing here touches the page's actual load-bearing claims (fibrosis regression
  timelines, the 78-week survival-curve separation, coffee/antioxidant/meat findings).
