**NOT RELEVANT** — concerns transplant-listing policy, which the page does not discuss and which is very unlikely to apply here given the stage 4 cancer diagnosis (active malignancy is ordinarily a transplant exclusion). Does not bear on the abstinence/survival-timeline or medication claims in doc 07.

### PMID 40991211 — Pharmacological therapies for AUD reduce decompensation & mortality in ALD
*Indian J Med Res, 2025; GRADE meta-analysis, 8 studies in meta-analysis*
All-cause mortality lower with AUD pharmacotherapy: OR 0.86 (95% CI 0.79–0.93), from 2 cohort studies, n=44,813. Pooled abstinence proportion 47.5% (95% CI 42.1–52.9) from 1 RCT + 3 cohorts, n=303. **Certainty rated very low (GRADE).**
**STRENGTHENS/EXTENDS** — doc 07's medication table currently rests on individual drug trials (baclofen RCT n=84; naltrexone safety n=3,285). This adds population-scale mortality evidence for AUD pharmacotherapy as a class, consistent with doc 07's framing. The very-low GRADE certainty and modest sample count for abstinence (n=303) mean this should be read as reinforcing, not upgrading, the existing claims — one more reason not to overstate.

### PMID 41631546 — Cochrane: aminoglycosides, vancomycin, metronidazole for HE
*Cochrane Database Syst Rev, 2026; Cochrane review, 24 RCTs, n=1,405*
Aminoglycosides **may slightly increase** mortality vs other active agents (RR 1.64, 95% CI 1.03–2.62; 3 studies, n=166) and serious adverse events (RR 1.60, 95% CI 1.03–2.47); evidence on vancomycin and metronidazole too uncertain to draw conclusions. Overall certainty low to very low; 22/24 trials at high risk of bias.
**STRENGTHENS/EXTENDS** — the page does not currently mention these older antibiotics at all (doc 06 covers lactulose, rifaximin, pro/synbiotics). This is new-to-the-page information, and it is consistent with — not contradictory to — the page's existing preference for lactulose/rifaximin: these alternatives are not shown to be better and one (aminoglycosides) carries a harm signal.

### PMID 41542879 — Cochrane: branched-chain amino acids for HE
*Cochrane Database Syst Rev, 2026; Cochrane review, 18 RCTs, n=934*
BCAAs reduce hepatic encephalopathy (RR 0.79, 95% CI 0.64–0.96; low-certainty evidence) but have **no significant effect on mortality** (RR 0.89, 95% CI 0.71–1.12; very-low-certainty evidence, 17 studies, n=867).
**STRENGTHENS/EXTENDS** — consistent with the page's general "protein/nutrition supports cirrhosis, don't restrict it" line (docs 02/08), and is honest about a null mortality result, which matches house rule 2. Adds a Cochrane-level synthesis specifically on BCAA-for-HE that the page doesn't currently cite.

### PMID 41285365 — Oral nutritional therapy in cirrhosis: systematic review and meta-analysis
*Clin Nutr ESPEN, 2026; meta-analysis, 18 RCTs, n=3,128*
Nutritional intervention (BCAAs, high-protein/high-calorie diets, HMB) reduced overt HE (RR 0.70, 95% CI 0.52–0.95, NNT 11), decompensation (RR 0.69, 95% CI 0.53–0.90, NNT 16), hospitalisation (RR 0.65, 95% CI 0.49–0.86, NNT 8), and mortality (RR 0.64, 95% CI 0.49–0.84, NNT 23). High heterogeneity noted; longer-duration studies showed weaker mortality benefit.
**STRENGTHENS/EXTENDS** — directly supports docs 02/08's "protein should go up, not down" message with a larger, more recent synthesis than what's currently cited. Good candidate for an added citation, not a correction.

### PMID 42402719 — Lactulose for prevention and treatment of hepatic encephalopathy
*Liver Int, 2026; meta-analysis, 17 RCTs, n=1,428*
Lactulose reduces overt HE incidence (RR 0.48, 95% CI 0.37–0.63; primary-prophylaxis subgroup RR 0.43, 95% CI 0.27–0.69). Bayesian analysis: high probability of reduced mortality, posterior RR range 0.72–0.88. No increase in adverse events (RR 0.87, 95% CI 0.64–1.17).
**STRENGTHENS/EXTENDS** — doc 06 already cites a Cochrane lactulose mortality figure (RR 0.59, 95% CI 0.40–0.87, 24 RCTs, n=1,487). This is a different, more recent meta-analysis (17 RCTs, n=1,428, partly Bayesian) whose mortality estimate (posterior range 0.72–0.88) is directionally the same but less dramatic than the existing citation. Both show significant benefit; the size differs somewhat by method and study set. Not a contradiction — one contradicting-in-degree study/review is not grounds to swap the existing figure — but worth a footnote if a person is editing doc 06 anyway.
