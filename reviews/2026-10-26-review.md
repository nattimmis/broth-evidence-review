## Papers reviewed

### PMID 42923263
- **Title:** Second-line treatment selection for advanced hepatocellular carcinoma: a network meta-analysis with reconstructed survival curves and integrated benefit-risk assessment.
- **Journal:** Frontiers in Immunology, 2026 Oct 8;17:1908072.
- **Design:** Network meta-analysis (frequentist random-effects) of 12 phase III RCTs (11 in the primary post-TKI network; REACH-2 contributed only to an AFP≥400 ng/mL exploratory subgroup), plus 7 observational studies synthesized separately for post-ICI strategies. Reconstructed individual patient data from published Kaplan-Meier curves; PROSPERO CRD420261329202.
- **n:** 12 RCTs in the main network (patient-level n not given in the abstract; UNVERIFIED without pulling the full paper).
- **Finding:** Among second-line agents after TKI failure, regorafenib, cabozantinib, and pembrolizumab each significantly improved overall survival vs. placebo/best supportive care, with no significant OS difference between them. In the AFP≥400 ng/mL subgroup, ramucirumab improved OS vs. placebo/BSC. Pembrolizumab had the most favorable benefit-risk profile (lowest exploratory multidimensional-distance-to-corner score, 0.439, vs. 0.693 for regorafenib and 0.908 for cabozantinib). Exploratory post-ICI analysis: regorafenib + ICI combination associated with improved OS vs. regorafenib monotherapy.
- **Classification: NOT RELEVANT.**
- **Reasoning:** This is a treatment-selection analysis for second-line systemic HCC therapy after TKI failure — an oncology drug-sequencing question, not a diet, lifestyle, or alcohol-abstinence question. The page makes no claims about chemotherapy/immunotherapy drug choice, so there is nothing here to strengthen or contradict. It was picked up only because it matched the broad `hcc` tracking topic (hepatocellular carcinoma systemic therapy & survival), which exists to watch the space, not because it bears on any specific claim in docs/01–12.
