# Evidence review — 2026-09-17

One new paper since the last review (PMID 42745769), and it does not change any conclusion on the page: it studies add-on diabetes drugs for MASLD (metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease) in an Asian T2DM population, which is a different aetiology from this person's alcohol-related cirrhosis, and its endpoints are short-term liver-enzyme/glycaemic surrogates rather than fibrosis or survival.

## PMID 42745769

- **Title**: Incremental effects of SGLT2 inhibitors, GLP-1 receptor agonists, and pioglitazone in type 2 diabetes and MASLD across Asian study settings: a systematic review and meta-analysis.
- **Journal**: Frontiers in Endocrinology (Lausanne), 2026 Sep 1;17:1942151.
- **Design**: Systematic review and meta-analysis of 11 RCTs (PROSPERO CRD420251231137).
- **n**: 692 randomized; 642 included in the coprimary efficacy analyses.
- **Finding**: In T2DM + MASLD patients, adding an SGLT2 inhibitor to background glucose-lowering therapy reduced ALT by a mean of −7.56 U/L (95% CI −11.24 to −3.88, moderate certainty) with no clinically important further HbA1c benefit (MD −0.09%, 95% CI −0.27 to 0.08, moderate certainty). GLP-1 receptor agonists gave a modest ALT reduction (MD −6.71 U/L, 95% CI −11.53 to −1.89, moderate certainty) and a low-certainty HbA1c reduction (MD −0.48%, 95% CI −0.83 to −0.14). Trials were short-term, comparative safety could not be assessed reliably (adverse-event reporting was heterogeneous/incomplete), and the authors explicitly say durability and clinical significance remain uncertain.
- **Classification**: **NOT RELEVANT**.
- **Reasoning**: Wrong aetiology and wrong population — this is metabolic/diabetes-driven steatotic liver disease (MASLD), not alcohol-related cirrhosis, and the endpoints (ALT, HbA1c) are short-term surrogate markers, not fibrosis regression or survival. Nothing here bears on abstinence-as-treatment, coffee, antioxidants, protein/fat, or probiotic-safety conclusions already on the page.
