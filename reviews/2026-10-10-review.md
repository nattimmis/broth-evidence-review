# Evidence review — 2026-10-10

One new paper since the last review (2026-10-08). It does not change any conclusion on
the page: it is a real-world, non-randomised comparison of two first-line HCC
immunotherapy regimens that the authors themselves say cannot be reliably compared for
survival, and the page makes no claim in that specific territory (which drug combo to
pick) for this to update.

## PMID 42851630

**Title:** Real-world outcomes of atezolizumab-bevacizumab and durvalumab-tremelimumab
in unresectable hepatocellular carcinoma: a systematic review with covariate-adjusted
meta-regression and exploratory machine learning analysis.

**Journal:** Frontiers in Immunology, 2026 Sep 24 (PMCID PMC13646090)

**Design:** Systematic review and meta-regression of 76 observational (real-world)
reports — 60 reports / 8,928 patients on atezolizumab-bevacizumab (AB), 16 reports /
1,315 patients on durvalumab-tremelimumab (DT). No randomisation; report-level, not
patient-level, data for most endpoints.

**Finding:** Sample-size-weighted median overall survival (OS): 17.7 months (AB) vs
16.5 months (DT), summarised descriptively and *not formally compared*. In the adjusted
meta-regression, the AB–DT difference was:
- OS: +3.57 months (95% CI −0.38 to +7.52, P = 0.076) — **not significant**
- Progression-free survival: +1.96 months (95% CI +0.91 to +3.02, P < 0.001) —
  significant, but not reproduced in first-line-predominant cohorts (+1.35 months,
  95% CI −0.33 to +3.04, P = 0.116) and attenuated after excluding overlapping reports
  (+1.69 months, P = 0.016)
- Objective response rate: +2.95 points (95% CI −2.65 to +8.54, P = 0.302) —
  **not significant**
- Disease control rate: +16.15 points (95% CI +10.44 to +21.86, P < 0.001) — significant

The authors' own conclusion: OS "could not be reliably compared" between the two
regimens, and differences in treatment line, age, calendar period, and the small DT
evidence base "preclude reliable comparative-effectiveness inference."

**Classification: NOT RELEVANT.**

**Reasoning:** This is the right disease (unresectable HCC) but the wrong question for
this page. The page (docs/13) notes in passing that the standard of care for first-line
HCC immunotherapy has moved (CheckMate 9DW, nivolumab+ipilimumab, OS 23.7 vs 20.6
months, HR 0.79) so the treating team can be checked against current numbers — but the
page does not take, and has no reason to take, a position on AB versus DT specifically;
that is a treating-oncologist decision, not a diet/lifestyle/root-cause claim this
repository audits. Even on its own terms, the paper is observational (no randomisation),
its primary survival comparison is non-significant, and the authors explicitly disclaim
reliable comparative inference because of confounding and a small, unbalanced evidence
base. There is nothing here a non-oncologist reviewer should turn into page text, and
nothing that contradicts anything already written.
