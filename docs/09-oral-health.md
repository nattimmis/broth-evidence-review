# 09 — The mouth: right instinct, wrong liquid

The proposal under test: swirling orange juice around the mouth as a health practice.
The idea that the mouth is a lever turns out to be **correct**. Orange juice is the
wrong way to pull it, and the real version has better evidence than most of this
repository.

## What is real about mouth rinsing

There is a genuine carbohydrate-mouth-rinse literature: swilling a carbohydrate
solution without swallowing improves endurance performance through oral receptors and
central drive rather than absorption. Meta-analysis effect **Hedges' g = 0.18**
(0.09–0.28) — real, and small. It lasts minutes, applies to fasted athletes mid-effort,
uses maltodextrin rather than fruit juice, and has **no liver, detox or cancer
relevance whatsoever**. No such trial exists.

## Why the swirling specifically is harmful

- Orange juice pH **3.8**, with the highest titratable acidity of common drinks — about
  three times as much neutraliser as cola to bring it back to neutral. Enamel
  demineralises below pH 5.5.
- Holding or swishing prolongs enamel contact and amplifies erosion. It is the single
  worst way to consume an acidic drink.
- Chemotherapy and radiotherapy cause dry mouth, removing the salivary buffering,
  clearance and remineralisation that would otherwise limit the damage.
- Cancer mouth-care guidance (MASCC/ISOO; Cancer Research UK) recommends bland
  **saline or sodium bicarbonate** rinses and explicitly advises **avoiding citrus
  juices**, which irritate sore mucosa.
- A glass carries **20–23 g of sugar**, and ordinary orange juice reduces absorption of
  some medicines for up to four hours (see [`10-interactions-and-traps.md`](10-interactions-and-traps.md)).

## The real lever: treat the gums

Gum disease is a reservoir of exactly the bacterial endotoxin a cirrhotic liver can no
longer clear.

**Non-surgical periodontal therapy in cirrhosis** (26 treated, 24 untreated controls,
30-day follow-up):

| Measure | Before → After | p |
|---|---|---|
| Endotoxin | 0.21 → **0.11 EU/mL** | 0.02 |
| IL-6 | 4.9 → 3.5 pg/mL | <0.05 |
| MELD score | 10.4 → **8.7** | <0.05 |
| Cognition (EncephalApp Stroop), HE patients | 217.3 → **194.1 s** | 0.03 |

Endotoxin and LPS-binding protein **rose** in the untreated group. Caveats: small,
unblinded, non-randomised, 30 days.

**Regular dental care in 47,809 people with cirrhosis** (retrospective, so healthy-user
bias applies): 27% lower odds of hepatocellular carcinoma, 20% lower liver-related
hospitalisation, 19% lower hepatic encephalopathy, 15% lower ascites. Only **17.5%**
received yearly cleanings.

Severe periodontitis and cirrhosis-related mortality: crude HR **2.19** (1.07–4.50),
n=184.

## The nitrate pathway — real, but not from orange juice

Oral bacteria reduce dietary nitrate to nitrite and onward to nitric oxide;
antibacterial mouthwash abolishes this and raises systolic blood pressure by 2–3.5
mmHg. Beetroot juice lowers systolic pressure by 3.55–5.31 mmHg. But orange juice
contains **13–30 mg/L** nitrate versus **over 2,000 mg/kg** in rocket and spinach.
If the goal is an oral-cavity effect, the food is leafy greens and beetroot.

## Verdict

Book the dentist, not the juice. This is cheap, harmless, badly missed, and has
faster measurable effects than anything else in this repository.
