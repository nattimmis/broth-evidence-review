# 10 — Interactions, safety traps and the sodium arithmetic

Specific, numbered, and mostly invisible. Several are things a well-meaning person
would do on purpose.

## 1. "Dark brown bread" is not automatically low-salt

Bread is the **single largest contributor of salt** in the UK diet — 14–17% of total
intake. In a survey of 242 pre-sliced breads, 75% carried ≥0.34 g salt per slice,
which is **136 mg sodium a slice**. Against the 2,000 mg/day ceiling in cirrhosis with
ascites: two slices is 13.6%, four slices is 27%. Brands vary about threefold.

**The colour tells you nothing** — the lowest-salt loaf in that survey was a dark rye
sourdough. Read **g salt per 100 g** on the pack and aim under 1.0.

## 2. Potassium salt substitutes

The obvious workaround for a salt ceiling is potassium chloride "low sodium salt".
With **spironolactone** — a potassium-sparing diuretic used for ascites — this is
contraindicated and can cause dangerous hyperkalaemia. Use acid, herbs and aromatics
instead.

## 3. Ordinary orange juice reduces drug absorption

Not just grapefruit. Via OATP transporter inhibition, orange juice **reduces**
exposure: celiprolol Cmax −89% and AUC **−83%**; fexofenadine AUC −30% to −77%. The
effect lasts over 2 hours but under 4 — so it is separable by timing.

## 4. Grapefruit and Seville orange

A different mechanism — CYP3A4 inhibition, which **increases** exposure. 250 mL of
grapefruit juice raised felodipine AUC **threefold**. Oncology trials routinely exclude
both.

## 5. St John's wort

Imatinib AUC **−32%**; SN-38 (irinotecan's active metabolite) exposure **−42% to −50%**.
It makes chemotherapy weaker. Contraindicated.

## 6. Live probiotics and kefir — the immunocompromised problem

See [`12-fermented-foods-and-blends.md`](12-fermented-foods-and-blends.md). Briefly:
probiotic use and invasive infection with the probiotic organism carried **OR 127** in a
matched case-control study, and **all 7 cases who had received kefir or probiotic
yoghurt grew Lactobacillus from a sterile site**. Guidance says probiotics are not
recommended for patients who are immunocompromised, critically ill, or have indwelling
catheters.

## 7. Green tea extract

Brewed tea is fine. Concentrated extract is NIH LiverTox **category A** — a
well-established cause of liver injury, with over 100 published cases including liver
failure, transplant and death.

## 8. Antioxidant supplements during chemotherapy

Recurrence HR **1.41** (p=0.06) in 1,134 patients, with no demonstrated benefit on the
other side. Food yes, capsules no.

## 9. Raw shellfish

Cirrhosis is the major risk factor for *Vibrio vulnificus* septicaemia, mortality on
the order of **50%**. Cooked shellfish is fine. Raw is not.

## 10. Listeria foods, and the raw-salad question

Raw sprouts, pre-cut bagged salad, pre-cut fruit and unpasteurised soft cheese are
named vehicles; **70%** of non-pregnancy listeriosis occurs in immunocompromised
people, with chronic liver disease an explicit risk state.

**Update — this genuinely moved.** The strict neutropenic diet had fallen out of favour
after three RCTs found no benefit. Then in **December 2025** a phase III non-inferiority
trial was **stopped early at interim analysis** (214 evaluable patients): the
liberalised diet exceeded the non-inferiority margin for major infection, greater
adherence to the liberal diet meant *higher* infection risk, and there was no
calorie or protein benefit. That trial was in stem-cell-transplant and acute-leukaemia
patients, **not stage 4 solid tumours**, so it does not settle the question either way.
**Ask the oncology team directly.**

## 11. Iron

About **half** of people with alcohol-related liver disease develop hepatic iron
overload — ethanol suppresses hepcidin, increasing duodenal absorption. Do not take
iron supplements or load iron-fortified foods unless blood tests say to.

## 12. Aflatoxin

IARC Group 1. Population-attributable fraction for hepatocellular carcinoma **17%**
(14–19%), and the risk **multiplies with alcohol**. EU limits are 2 µg/kg for B1.
Regulated retail nuts are low risk; discard anything shrivelled, discoloured or musty,
and avoid unregulated bulk imports.

## 13. Potassium, when the bloods say so

Orange juice, bananas, potatoes, avocado and leafy greens are all high in potassium.
On spironolactone, or with impaired kidneys, concern starts around a serum level of
**5.0–5.5 mmol/L**. This is bloods-driven, not a blanket ban — but somebody must
actually be checking.

## 14. Vitamin K and warfarin

The rule is **consistency, not avoidance** — roughly 90–120 µg/day held steady.
Patients with unstable INR had *lower* vitamin K intake, and supplementation reduced
INR variability.
