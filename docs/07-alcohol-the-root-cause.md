# 07 — Alcohol: the root cause, and what stopping is actually worth

Where alcohol is the cause, abstinence is not a lifestyle recommendation sitting
alongside the diet. **It is the treatment**, and everything else is support for it.

## What changes, and when

| Endpoint | Change | Timeframe |
|---|---|---|
| Liver stiffness | 9.2 → **6.9 kPa**; 80% of abstainers improved. Continued drinkers **+2.2 kPa** | **4 weeks** |
| Steatosis | resolves | 2–4 weeks |
| Portal pressure (HVPG) | 19 → **9 mmHg**, a 44% fall, in those who recompensated | ≥3 months |
| Decompensation | adjusted HR **0.391**; 3-yr decompensation 32.4% vs 60.0% | 36 months |
| Liver-related death | adjusted HR **0.428**; all-cause HR 0.453 | 36 months |
| Recompensation (Baveno VII) | **12.3%** at 1 yr → 23.4% at 2 yr → **33.8%** at 5 yr | years |

Caveat on the fastest number: most of a 4-week liver-stiffness drop is inflammation
settling, not scar tissue regressing.

## The 78-week problem

**Survival curves for abstinent and still-drinking patients do not separate until
about 1.5 years** (meta-analysis, 7 cohorts, n=1,235 — no difference at 6 or 12
months, significant from 18 months, HR ~0.51–0.63 thereafter).

This is the single most under-appreciated fact in alcohol-related liver disease.
Someone stops, feels no reward, and starts again just before the benefit was about to
appear. **37% relapse within the first 90 days** on blood-test-verified measurement
(self-report understates it badly: 56% claimed vs 37% actual).

Seven-year survival: **72% abstinent vs 44% continuing** (n=100, biopsy-proven).
Abstinence at one month post-diagnosis was the dominant predictor — biopsy severity
was not.

## After a cancer diagnosis

- Post-resection overall survival at 3/5/7 years: **91/76/66% abstinent vs 87/57/44%**.
  Non-abstinence an independent predictor of death, **HR 2.24**.
- **Recurrence is NOT reduced** (p=0.71). Abstinence buys liver function, not tumour
  control. This must not be overstated.
- HCC *prevention* benefit may already be closed once decompensated: abstinence reduced
  HCC in compensated disease (HR 0.35) but not decompensated (HR 0.95).

## Medication — the part most often missed

| Drug | Evidence in cirrhosis | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **Baclofen** | RCT in cirrhosis, n=84, 12 weeks: abstinence **71% vs 29%** placebo (OR 6.3). No hepatic side effects | **The only agent with randomised proof in cirrhosis** |
| **Naltrexone** | 3,285 cirrhosis initiations: 2% enzyme elevations, **zero cases meeting drug-injury criteria** | The old hepatotoxicity warning has largely not held up |
| **Acamprosate** | Renally excreted, LiverTox category E (lowest risk), no published liver-injury cases | Safe; efficacy in cirrhosis untested against placebo |
| **Disulfiram** | Fully hepatically metabolised, hepatotoxic | **Contraindicated** |

A systematic review across 23 studies and 104,298 patients found baclofen the only
intervention with a statistically significant randomised effect. Psychological therapy
alone did not reach significance (abstinence OR 1.87, 0.38–9.23).

**Integrated hepatology + addiction care** cut alcohol-related readmission from
**32% to 18% at 30 days** and 49% to 35% at 90 days — without changing early remission
rates. It changes hard endpoints, not willpower.

## Is any amount safe?

No. EASL recommends complete abstinence in all patients with cirrhosis, with no
threshold. Empirically the risk is dose-dependent from the first drink: in 650
patients with biopsy-proven compensated alcohol-related cirrhosis, risk rose from
**one glass-year**, and benefit was demonstrable below one glass per week.
