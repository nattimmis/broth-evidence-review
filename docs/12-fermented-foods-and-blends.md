# 12 — Kefir, blends and the question of pace

A proposal arrived for a daily breakfast blend: kefir, orange juice, kiwi, turmeric,
avocado, banana and leafy greens, taken to fill the stomach and intestines, with the
body framed as a bioreactor.

**The bioreactor framing is the best one anybody has offered.** The colon genuinely is
a fermenter. The proposal also contains the single most dangerous item reviewed
anywhere in this repository, and the question "at what maximum pace" has an answer
that matters more than the recipe.

---

## 1. Kefir — the one to stop

Live fermented milk in a person who is immunocompromised from cancer treatment *and*
cirrhotic. Cirrhosis already causes bacterial translocation across a leaky gut, with
portal-systemic bypass — the exact route by which a swallowed organism reaches the
bloodstream.

| Finding | Number | Design |
|---|---|---|
| Probiotic use vs invasive infection **with the probiotic organism** | **OR 127** (6.21–2,600); *Lactobacillus* 68% of isolates | matched case-control, 28 cases/84 controls |
| *S. boulardii* use and *Saccharomyces* fungaemia | **OR 14** (4–44); mortality **37% at day 28** | registry, 5 hospitals, population 3.29M |
| *Lactobacillus* bacteraemia severity | 333 patients; endocarditis 19%; mortality **27% at 90 days** | case series |
| Probiotics in critically ill patients | mortality **16% vs 6%**; bowel ischaemia 9 vs 0 | PROPATRIA RCT, n=296 |

**This is not only about capsules.** In the case-control study, **all 7 cases who had
received *Lactobacillus*-containing kefir or probiotic yoghurt grew *Lactobacillus*
from a sterile site.** Ordinary fermented food produced the organism.

There is also a published case of probiotic-strain *L. rhamnosus* endocarditis in a
**cirrhotic patient**. Guidance cited by CDC's journal states probiotics are "not
recommended for patients who have indwelling catheters, are immunocompromised, or are
critically ill" — this person plausibly meets all three at different times.

**And the benefit side is thin.** No kefir trial in cirrhosis exists. The only kefir
liver RCT (NAFLD, n=80, 500 ml/day, 8 weeks) was **negative** for liver enzymes.
Probiotics generally, in cirrhosis: recovery RR 0.67 and ammonia −8.29 µmol/L, but
**mortality RR 0.58 — not significant** (Cochrane, 21 RCTs, n=1,420). Any encephalopathy
benefit is already better served by lactulose and rifaximin, which are
guideline-recommended.

There is also a possible-harm signal with immunotherapy: probiotic users had response
59% vs 68% and progression-free survival 17 vs 23 months — **not statistically
significant**, and reported here as a signal, not a finding.

**Verdict: no. Not without explicit oncology sign-off.** The dairy protein kefir
provides — about 3.8 g/100 ml, roughly 9–10 g a glass — is genuinely valuable and EASL
prefers dairy protein. **That can be had from pasteurised, non-live dairy at zero
infection cost.** If someone insists: commercial pasteurised-milk product only, never
artisanal or raw, never during neutropenia or with a central line.

## 2. The blend — a good idea, for a reason nobody expects

The case for it is **not** vitamins. It is that **liquid calories are poorly
compensated for.** A systematic review of 253 preload interventions found energy
compensation was weakest for liquids — people do not eat correspondingly less at the
next meal. For a person struggling to eat, that failure of satiety is precisely the
point. It is the same logic as the broth: a vehicle.

- **Blending retains fibre; juicing removes it.** Blended fruit gave *lower* glucose
  excursion than whole fruit in a randomised crossover (n=20): peak 28.8 vs 42.5 mg/dL,
  iAUC 850 vs 1,269 (p=0.005). Commercial smoothies measured low-GI at 39 and 36.
- **Oral nutritional supplements work**: in malnourished cancer patients (13 RCTs,
  n=1,414) they added **+432 kcal/day** and **+1.86 kg** weight, improved quality of
  life, and showed **no survival benefit** — both effects lost significance in
  sensitivity analysis. Take them for intake and comfort, not for survival.
- **Kiwifruit is a defensible extra.** Two green kiwifruit a day beat psyllium on
  complete spontaneous bowel movements (+1.69 vs +0.90/week, p=0.038; RCT crossover,
  n=184). But meta-analysis (7 RCTs, n=399) puts kiwi vs psyllium at MD +1.01 (−0.02 to
  2.04) and **vs placebo at +1.36 (−0.44 to 3.16) — confidence intervals crossing
  zero.** As good as psyllium, not proven against nothing.
- **Do not put banana with berries or cocoa.** Banana is high in polyphenol oxidase. In
  a crossover (n=8) a banana smoothie cut flavan-3-ol peak concentration by **84%** and
  AUC by **81%** versus capsules; a berry smoothie matched capsules. Blending banana
  with the polyphenols you are adding for their own sake destroys them.
- **Fructose:** banana + 200 ml orange juice + 2 kiwi is roughly **20–22 g fructose**,
  against the 80 g/day dose that doubled hepatic de-novo lipogenesis in a 7-week trial
  (n=94). About a quarter of that, eaten with fibre — acceptable, not free.
- Vitamin C loss on standing is negligible (half-life 366–1,045 minutes), so "drink it
  within 30 minutes" is about taste, not nutrients.
- **No trial of a homemade blend exists in liver disease or cancer.**

**So the blend should be a protein-and-energy vehicle:** a dairy base (pasteurised,
non-live), nut butter or oil for density, two green kiwi, and fruit for palatability.
Not juice-based, not fruit-only, never a meal replacement, and always **between** meals
rather than instead of one.

## 3. Pace — the part that actually matters

The question was "at which maximum pace". The answer is **much slower than expected at
the start, and the reason is that going fast can kill.**

### Refeeding syndrome

Reintroducing carbohydrate to a depleted person drives insulin, which pushes phosphate,
potassium and magnesium into cells and consumes thiamine. The result can be arrhythmia,
respiratory and cardiac failure, seizures and death.

**NICE lists "a history of alcohol abuse or drugs including insulin, chemotherapy,
antacids or diuretics" as a high-risk criterion.** Alcohol, chemotherapy and
spironolactone are all on that list. This person plausibly qualifies as high-risk on
that clause alone.

The prescribed rate:

| | Rate |
|---|---|
| Day 1, high risk | **maximum 10 kcal/kg/day** |
| Extreme cases (BMI <14, or negligible intake >15 days) | **5 kcal/kg/day**, with cardiac monitoring |
| Time to reach full requirement | **4–7 days** |
| ASPEN alternative | 100–150 g dextrose or 10–20 kcal/kg for 24 h, advancing by **33% of goal every 1–2 days** |

**EASL's 35 kcal/kg/day and 1.2–1.5 g protein/kg/day are the destination, not the
starting speed.** Electrolytes are replaced alongside: potassium 2–4 mmol/kg/day,
phosphate 0.3–0.6, magnesium 0.2 IV or 0.4 oral.

### Thiamine goes first

Oral thiamine **200–300 mg daily**, immediately before and during the first 10 days of
feeding, plus B-complex and a multivitamin. ESPEN: give a first dose *before*
commencing nutrition to prevent Wernicke's encephalopathy. The honest nuance: the
evidence that a single glucose dose precipitates Wernicke's is weak — the real hazard
is prolonged carbohydrate without thiamine.

### Volume is capped by the ascites, not by willpower

**"Stuffing the stomach" is counterproductive.** AASLD notes ascites and oedema "lead
to early satiety". Both AASLD and EASL therefore recommend **small, frequent meals
every 3–4 hours** — three meals plus three snacks, a protein-containing breakfast, and
a late evening snack. A large single volume reduces total daily intake.

### Fibre

UK target **30 g/day** against an average intake of 18 g. Increase **gradually, with
fluid** — no national guideline gives a numeric increment, and the commonly quoted
"2–3 g/day" figures are not from a guideline. Too fast simply buys bloating, which
suppresses intake.

### Potassium — the blend's real hazard

A large greens-plus-banana-plus-avocado blend can add **1,500–2,500 mg** of potassium
on top of the diet, against a normal daily intake target of roughly 3,500 mg.

On spironolactone, with reduced renal perfusion, that is not trivial:

| Serum potassium | Status |
|---|---|
| 5.5–5.9 mmol/L | mild hyperkalaemia |
| ≥6.0 | moderate — ECG and cardiac monitoring; EASL: stop anti-mineralocorticoids at >6.0 |
| ≥6.5 | severe — urgent treatment |

Reference loads: baked potato with skin **919 mg**, ½ cup cooked spinach **591 mg**,
small banana **362 mg**.

This is bloods-driven, not a blanket ban. But somebody must be checking, and the
blend is a real potassium dose rather than a neutral one.

### Fluid

Not routinely restricted. EASL restricts to **1,000 ml/day only for hypervolaemic
hyponatraemia** (sodium <130 mmol/L), and diuretics are stopped below 125. A daily
blend is otherwise not a fluid problem.

## Summary

| Item | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Kefir, live | **No** without oncology sign-off — infection risk, and food has caused it |
| Dairy protein | **Yes** — from pasteurised, non-live dairy |
| A daily blend | **Yes**, as an energy-and-protein vehicle between meals |
| Kiwifruit ×2 | Reasonable for constipation; as good as psyllium, unproven vs placebo |
| Banana + berries together | **No** — banana destroys the berry polyphenols |
| Orange juice | Poor choice — sugar, drug interactions, dental erosion, potassium |
| Turmeric, culinary | Fine. Supplements and piperine, no |
| "Stuffing" the stomach | **Counterproductive** — small frequent meals instead |
| Starting at full calories | **Dangerous** — 10 kcal/kg/day, build over 4–7 days |
| Thiamine | **Before the calories**, 200–300 mg daily for at least 10 days |
