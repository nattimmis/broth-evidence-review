# 03 — Food safety for this specific person

Two compromised systems at once: a cirrhotic liver and, likely, treatment-related
immunosuppression. A few ordinary foods stop being ordinary.

## The hard rule: no raw shellfish

**Cirrhosis is the major risk factor for *Vibrio vulnificus* septicaemia, and the
mortality is high — on the order of 50%.** Infection follows eating raw or
undercooked shellfish, classically oysters, or exposing a wound to seawater. Iron
overload and impaired hepatic clearance in liver disease make cirrhotic patients
uniquely vulnerable to fulminant disease.

This is the most dangerous item likely to appear on a "Mediterranean" table, and it
is the reason the recipe file carries the rule in bold. **Cooked shellfish is fine.
Raw is not.** No oysters, no ceviche, no raw or lightly-marinated seafood.

## Lead in bone broth

Real, measured, and modest. Worth knowing rather than worrying about.

Monro et al. found bone broths contain **several times the lead concentration of the
water used to make them** — chicken bone broth at **7.01 µg/L**, and broth from skin
and cartilage at **9.5 µg/L**. Bone sequesters lead over an animal's life, and long
simmering with acid leaches some of it out.

Counterweight: a later analysis of essential and toxic metals in animal bone broths
found the risk from Pb and Cd **minimal**, at a few µg per serving — and noted that
the calcium and magnesium people drink broth *for* also come in at under 5% of daily
recommended levels per serving. The mineral case for bone broth is weaker than
folklore claims, and so is the lead case against it.

**Practical position:** broth is fine as one nourishing item among several. It is not
a reason for alarm, and not a food to live on exclusively. Sourcing bones from
pasture-raised animals and not extending beyond ~13h both help at the margin.

## Immunocompromised handling

The strict "neutropenic diet" had largely fallen out of favour — three RCTs found no
reduction in infection while it *did* reduce intake, which is the greater danger.

> **Update, December 2025 — this is no longer settled.** A phase III non-inferiority
> trial was **stopped early at interim analysis** (214 evaluable patients): the
> liberalised diet exceeded the non-inferiority margin for major infection, greater
> adherence to the liberal diet meant *higher* infection risk, and there was no calorie
> or protein benefit. That trial was in stem-cell-transplant and acute-leukaemia
> patients, **not stage 4 solid tumours**, so it does not settle the question either
> way. Ask the oncology team rather than trusting any general guidance, including this.
> See [`10-interactions-and-traps.md`](10-interactions-and-traps.md).

Ordinary food-safety discipline remains the floor, and it is sensible regardless:

| Do | Why |
|---|---|
| Wash all produce thoroughly | Raw salad is fine; unwashed is not |
| Pasteurised dairy only | Soft unpasteurised cheeses carry listeria risk |
| Cool broth fast, refrigerate within 2h | A 4 L pot of stock is an excellent bacterial incubator |
| Reheat to steaming, once, per portion | Freeze in cubes so nothing is reheated repeatedly |
| Cook shellfish and eggs through | See above |
| Respect the 5-day fridge limit | Don't push it for a compromised person |

Cooling is the underrated one. A large pot left on the stove overnight to "cool
naturally" spends hours in the danger zone. Decant into shallow containers, or sit
the pot in an ice bath, then refrigerate.

**Sources:** [Monro et al., lead contamination in bone broth diets](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23375414/) ·
[Essential and toxic metals in animal bone broths](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5533136/)
