# 06 — The gut–liver axis, and the "sponge" claim

The proposal under test: that porridge and fruit act like a sponge, absorbing toxins
from the intestines. This one deserves care, because **the instinct behind it is
correct and the mechanism is wrong**, and the correct part is genuinely important.

## The instinct is right: there *is* a toxin problem, and it lives in the gut

In cirrhosis the intestinal barrier leaks. Bacterial products — principally
lipopolysaccharide (LPS) — cross into the portal circulation, and a failing liver
clears less of what arrives (roughly 40–50% of circulating LPS is normally cleared
hepatically). LPS then drives TNF-α through Kupffer cells and induces nitric oxide
synthase, worsening splanchnic vasodilation and portal hypertension. Small-intestinal
bacterial overgrowth is found in up to **59%** of cirrhotic patients.

Ammonia is the other half. So the destination of the "sponge" idea — *the gut is
where the toxin problem is* — is accurate.

## The mechanism is wrong: nothing in food absorbs a toxin

- Oat **beta-glucan binds bile acids**, which is why ≥3 g/day lowers LDL cholesterol
  by about 0.25 mmol/L. That is a real, specific, well-evidenced effect. It is not
  toxin binding.
- Fermentation to short-chain fatty acids is real, and butyrate supplies the majority
  of colonocyte energy and upregulates tight-junction proteins — but that evidence is
  in vitro and animal. **That eating oats measurably repairs the gut barrier in
  cirrhosis is unproven.**
- **No trial of oats exists in cirrhosis at all.**
- "Detox" in the commercial sense has no referent: a systematic review found **no
  randomised controlled trials** of detox diets in humans.

## What actually empties that pool

This is the part that matters, because it is where the attention should go.

| Intervention | Effect | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Lactulose** | mortality **RR 0.59** (0.40–0.87) | Cochrane, 24 RCTs, n=1,487 |
| **Rifaximin** | breakthrough encephalopathy **22.1% vs 45.9%** over 6 months, HR 0.42 | RCT, n=299 |
| Pre/pro/synbiotics | no-recovery RR 0.67; ammonia **−8.29 µmol/L**; **mortality not significant** | Cochrane, 21 RCTs, n=1,420 |

Lactulose works by exactly the mechanism the sponge metaphor is groping for — it
acidifies the colon so ammonia is trapped as non-diffusible ammonium and carried out.
Onset of laxation is within hours; clinical improvement in encephalopathy typically
**24–48 hours**.

A qualifier worth keeping: a substantial share of ammonia comes from small-intestinal
and renal glutaminase rather than colonic bacteria, so colon-targeted measures cannot
capture all of it.

## Protein source

EASL: protein **1.2–1.5 g/kg/day**, and "encourage the consumption of vegetables and
dairy protein". In a small crossover trial (n=8), switching 50 g of animal protein to
vegetable protein moved nitrogen balance from **−1.7 to +0.2 g/day**. The evidence for
vegetable-over-animal is weak — EASL itself notes the benefit may be the accompanying
fibre rather than the protein — and the far more important instruction is **do not
restrict protein**.

## Verdict

Porridge is a good breakfast and a good vehicle for protein and calories. It is not a
sponge, it does not remove toxins, and it is not a substitute for lactulose or
rifaximin. Anyone reaching for the sponge idea should be pointed at the drugs that
actually do the job.
