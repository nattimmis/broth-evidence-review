# 14 - The shopping list

Everything in this repository turned into one trolley, for a large-format Co-op with an
in-store bakery. Quantities are for one adult of roughly 70 kg for about a week, worked
back from the targets: **2,450 kcal/day, 84-105 g protein/day, under 2 g sodium, building
toward 30 g fibre**, across three meals and three snacks.

## The four items originally asked for

| Item | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Ginger** | **Buy it** | Real trial evidence - for nausea, not cleansing |
| **Watermelon** | Leave it | 0.30 kcal/g. Fills the stomach, displaces the calories he needs |
| **Fresh orange juice** | Walk past | 496 mg potassium and 20.8 g sugar a glass, blocks drug absorption, erodes enamel, no liver benefit |
| **"Liver cleansers"** | Not a category | The liver charities say so themselves, and supplements cause ~20% of drug-induced liver injury |

### Ginger - the genuine find

- **Ryan et al. 2012, n=576 analysed:** 0.5-1.0 g/day, started **3 days BEFORE**
  chemotherapy, reduced acute nausea. **1.5 g was not better.**
- **Crichton 2024, n=103:** delayed vomiting **2% vs 23%**, delayed nausea 49% vs 79%,
  and **malnutrition 18% vs 41%** (p=0.032).
- **Meta-analysis, 35 RCTs:** grade-3 acute nausea RR **0.19**.
- **Warfarin:** randomised crossover (n=12) found **no measurable interaction** at these
  doses - INR AUC ratio 1.01.
- **In cirrhosis: NO EVIDENCE.** The liver data is NAFLD only and weak. Buy it for nausea.

### Watermelon - why a healthy food is the wrong food here

**30 kcal per 100 g; calorie density 0.30 kcal/g.** Reaching 500 kcal needs **1.67 kg**.
A whole wedge is 86 kcal. With ascites and early satiety, volume is the limiting
resource, and watermelon spends it. Potassium is genuinely fine (112 mg/100 g, low for
fruit). No human evidence for liver, purification or cancer.

### Orange juice

248 ml glass = **112 kcal, 496 mg potassium, 20.8 g sugar**. Cut exposure to celiprolol
by **83%** (crossover, n=10). Measured pH **3.66-3.75**; both fresh and processed juice
eroded human enamel. The one NAFLD trial put every arm on a weight-loss diet with no
juice-free control - uninterpretable. **Eat the orange instead.**

### The detox category, in the liver charities' own words

**Liver UK:** *"There is no quick way to cleanse or detox your liver... They do not build
up in your liver so there is no need to 'detox' it."* and *"There are no supplements that
have been proven to keep your liver healthy or to help treat liver disease. In fact, some
supplements can cause liver damage. You are at more risk of this if you already have a
liver condition."*

Herbal and dietary supplements cause about **20% of US drug-induced liver injury**.
**Milk thistle** failed a randomised trial (n=154, 24 weeks: 3.8% placebo vs 4.0% vs
3.8%) and a Cochrane review of 18 trials in 1,088 patients found no mortality effect.
Lemon water, dandelion, apple cider vinegar and celery juice have **no human liver
trials at all**.

## What 100 g of protein a day looks like

Verified figures only, ordinary food, no powders:

| Item | Portion | Protein |
|---|---|---|
| Chicken breast, roasted | 150 g | 44.7 g |
| Salmon, cooked (or 2 tins sardines) | 100 g | 22.1 g |
| Greek yoghurt, plain | 150 g pot | 15.4 g |
| Eggs | two | 12.6 g |
| Seeded farmhouse bread | two slices | 8.8 g |
| **Total** | | **103.7 g** |

Target for a 70 kg adult is 84-105 g. The two slices of bread contribute only **259 mg
sodium** - about 13% of the daily ceiling.

## The trolley

**Non-negotiables:** porridge oats 1 kg (60 g/day delivers 3 g beta-glucan; a 40 g
serving gives only ~2 g), whole milk 4-6 pints, plain **pasteurised** Greek yoghurt
2 x 500 g, coffee.

**Protein:** chicken breast 1 kg, tinned sardines or mackerel 4-6 tins (spring water or
oil, not brine), eggs 12-18, cheddar or cottage cheese, red lentils or no-added-salt
beans.

**Calorie density:** peanut butter, olive oil, double cream or full-fat milk powder for
fortifying without adding volume.

**Fruit and veg:** kiwifruit x14 (two a day), **kale, cabbage, broccoli - not spinach or
chard**, frozen berries, lemons/garlic/herbs/black pepper, fresh or ground ginger.

**Read the label:** bread - compare **g salt per 100 g**, aim under 0.9, under 0.8 is
genuinely good. Co-op's Ancient Grains Seeded Farmhouse is **0.81 g/100 g with 11 g
protein** and qualifies. Colour tells you nothing.

**Not in that shop:** **Ceylon cinnamon** - supermarket "cinnamon" is cassia, at roughly
a hundred times the coumarin (cassia 2,239-4,167 mg/kg vs true cinnamon 0-68). Order a
species-named *Cinnamomum zeylanicum* or skip it. **Oral nutritional supplements** - ask
the GP or dietitian for prescribable Ensure/Fortisip/Fresubin rather than buying retail;
Nurishment (20 g protein/can) and Meritene (9.3 g/serving) are the retail fallbacks.

## The aisles to walk past

Kefir and live yoghurt; spinach and chard for a daily raw blend; plain "cinnamon";
processed meat; potassium salt substitutes (contraindicated with spironolactone); the
entire supplement aisle - turmeric capsules, green tea extract, milk thistle, antioxidant
pills; bagged pre-cut salad, sprouts, pre-cut fruit and unpasteurised cheese; grapefruit
and Seville orange.

## The item that matters most, and nobody sells it

**The late-evening snack.** A meta-analysis of 8 trials in 341 patients found it improved
albumin, lowered ammonia, shortened prothrombin time and reduced rates of ascites and
encephalopathy. Toast and cheese, yoghurt and fruit, milk and oats - every night, before
bed. In a 12-month randomised trial the same supplement built body protein **at night**
and did nothing during the day.
